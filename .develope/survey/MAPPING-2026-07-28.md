BriefingRoom limitations are explicit:

- `landMasses`/`waters` are sea-mask planning geometry, not rectangular map
  bounds or terrain-height, land-class, collision, road, or placement proof;
- stand parking provides mission `crossroad_idx`/`slot_name` plus `x/y`, but no
  heading or per-stand elevation; airbase reference elevation stays separate;
- terminal fallback records have `Term_Index` and exact exported elevation but
  no slot name, heading, or dimensions;
- spawn types and points are planning data, not safe unit/formation/route
  placement;
- climate/INI values are excluded as weather authority because their
  provenance and field semantics are not sufficiently reliable.

Current installed/runtime or same-version real-MIZ exact evidence remains
higher authority when available. Otherwise, for each airport and then each
slot independently, the audit uses the exact record from the selected primary
upstream provider and falls back to an exact secondary-provider record only
when the primary record is unavailable. It never merges fields across
providers. For a dual-source terrain pydcs is normally primary and
BriefingRoom is the secondary cross-check; for the three BriefingRoom-only IDs,
BriefingRoom is the available primary snapshot. Every airport/slot fallback is
reported, and dual-source name, position, dimension, and plane/helicopter
capability differences remain explicit warnings.

The pydcs parser also preserves each airport's `slot_version`; a missing
declaration normalizes to upstream default version 1. Version 1 follows the
upstream large-slot/helicopter classification and does not consult slot
dimensions for normal planes. Version 2 requires aircraft length, width, and
height each to be strictly less than the slot dimensions, treats a missing
slot height as 1000 m, consults the category-capable flag, and ignores the
large-slot class. BriefingRoom exports have no pydcs resolver semantics, so
their dimensions and capability flags remain diagnostics rather than an exact
resolver-compatible claim.

## Remaining hard boundary

The following still require version-matched runtime evidence or new product
facilities:

- complete initialized unit and task-capability registries;
- complete store-to-station compatibility;
- complete per-terrain runway and parking registries;
- Mission Editor resave;
- DCS mission load and gameplay validation;
- campaign generation;
- automatic natural-language scenario planning.

A generated archive may be described as built and statically verified only.
Runtime validity remains unknown until an explicitly authorized DCS workflow
actually loads it.
